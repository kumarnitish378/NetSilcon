## Is NetSilcon a real chip?

Not yet. NetSilcon-6x4 is a working **FPGA prototype** on a Sipeed Tang Nano 20K, verified on real hardware. The QFN-24 package, the 3.3 V / 2.5 mA figures, SPI and on-chip flash are design targets for production silicon, and are labelled **T** on this site.

## What do M, E and T mean?

- **M** · measured on the FPGA reference implementation
- **E** · estimated by design tools (such as Gowin's power analysis) or from a datasheet
- **T** · a design target for production silicon, not built yet

## Why such a small network?

170 parameters is enough for sensor-level decisions: a fault check from four readings, a water/skip decision, a driving command from four distances. Small means fast, predictable and cheap. It is not meant for images, audio or language. A larger shape, NetSilcon-64x6 (16 → 64×6 → 16), is in design.

## How is it different from running the network on a microcontroller?

A microcontroller runs the network as instructions, one multiplication after another. On a Cortex-M4 the same network takes about 2,300 instructions, 14–21 µs at 168 MHz. NetSilcon computes all neurons of a layer at once and finishes in 15 clocks, 0.556 µs at 27 MHz, every time. See [Benchmarks](/benchmarks/).

## Is a laptop CPU faster?

Yes. On a network this small, one laptop core is about 5× faster. It also draws 10–55 W and needs an operating system. NetSilcon is meant to sit beside a microcontroller, not to replace a PC.

## Is the hardware result exactly the same as the software model?

Yes, bit for bit. Each model ships with 576 golden test vectors, and all 576 match on the FPGA for each of the three jobs. In hardware-in-the-loop, every one of 9,600 decisions matched the Python model.

## What happens if a model upload goes wrong?

Nothing bad. The upload goes into a staging buffer and is only swapped in if its CRC-16 is correct. A damaged or interrupted upload is refused, and the running model keeps working.

## Does an uploaded model survive power-off?

Not on the FPGA reference: uploaded models live in registers and the built-in model comes back at power-up. Flash storage is next on the roadmap.

## Can I use a different network shape?

Not on NetSilcon-6x4: the shape is the fixed part. Generating hardware for your own shape and your own FPGA is the plan for [NetSilcon-Studio](/studio/).

## Can I use it in a safety-critical system?

Not as the only protection. The datasheet states that NetSilcon-6x4 is not designed or certified as the sole protection in life-support, automotive, aviation or other safety-critical systems. Use it beside proper safety logic, for example ANDed with the MCU's enable.
