One FPGA bitstream. Three different products. No re-synthesis.

In NetSilcon-6x4, the hardware only knows the network's shape. The numbers arrive later, over USB:

- 144 weights (int8), 26 biases (int16) and 5 scaling shifts: **201 bytes** in total
- plus a CRC-16, sent in about 31 ms

The same board became, in turn:

- a BLDC **motor-fault detector** (current, voltage, speed, temperature)
- an **irrigation controller** (soil moisture, air temperature, humidity, sunlight), 97.4 % accurate
- a **rover driver** (4 LiDAR distances in, speed and steering out)

## Two design choices that paid off

1. **The upload goes into a staging RAM first.** Only if the CRC is correct is it copied into the network, in 7.4 µs. I sent a model with one wrong CRC bit on purpose: refused, and the old model kept running.
2. **The scaling shifts are loadable too**, not just the weights. Each model brings its own number range.

## The price of flexibility

Programmability has a cost. While nothing could write the weights, synthesis turned them into constants and the design used 21 % of the FPGA's logic. With weights, biases and shifts truly writable, it uses 49 %. That gap is the measured silicon cost of "one chip, many jobs".
